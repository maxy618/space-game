## 3. Relativistic dynamics and the speed of light

The game must distinguish between two different concepts:

- The engine's acceleration limit.
- The fundamental speed limit of the spacecraft.

The values 4000 for the regular engine and 6000 for boost are limits on engine-driven acceleration. They are not limits on the spacecraft's total velocity.

Gravitational maneuvers and other external forces must be able to accelerate the spacecraft beyond 6000. Disabling boost must not reset its existing velocity or artificially slow it down to 4000.

However, a spacecraft with nonzero mass must never reach or exceed the speed of light.

Research and implement an approach based on relativistic momentum:

`p = γ * m0 * v`

`γ = 1 / sqrt(1 - v² / C_GAME²)`

For recovering velocity, investigate the numerically stable inverse relationship:

`v = C_GAME * p / sqrt((m0 * C_GAME)² + |p|²)`

These are mathematical starting points, not instructions to mechanically insert formulas. Verify that they are correctly applied within the existing integrator, including vector direction, momentum changes under applied forces, and interaction with existing constraints.

The spacecraft's rest mass must remain constant. Do not artificially make its mass depend on velocity unless the chosen model actually requires it.

### Choosing C_GAME

Do not assign an arbitrary value to the speed of light.

Define it in the same unit system actually used by the simulation.

Choose a value that ensures:
- The current speed range up to 4000 and 6000 behaves practically the same as it does now.
- Movement at ordinary speeds retains its existing feel.
- The transition into relativistic behavior is gradual.
- Approaching the speed limit becomes noticeable at high velocities.
- Velocity always remains strictly below `C_GAME`.

60,000 game units per second can be considered as one candidate scenario, but it must not be treated as the correct value in advance.

Mathematically compare several candidates. Independently choose the best one based on the scale of the system, existing parameters, and intended gameplay feel.

Do not change `4000`, `6000`, or other existing gameplay values merely because a new speed limit has been introduced.

### Gravity

Pay particular attention to `Universe.updateAll()` and every other place where gravitational forces are applied.

Verify:
- Dimensional consistency.
- Correct acceleration calculations.
- That spacecraft mass does not incorrectly affect its gravitational acceleration.
- Dependence on the gravitational source's mass and distance.
- That gravity is not disabled prematurely near a star.
- Interaction between gravity and relativistic momentum.

Do not arbitrarily increase `GRAVITATION`.

Gravity must continue accelerating the spacecraft beyond 4000 and 6000, and the engine speed limiter must not cancel or truncate gravitational acceleration.

Investigate gravitational maneuvers involving moving planets and orbital energy exchange. Verify that they can actually change the spacecraft's velocity relative to the star system.

For the current game, a relativistic spacecraft moving within the existing Newtonian gravitational field is an acceptable approximation. However, do not describe this as full general relativity. Account for the limitations of this approximation, especially near strong gravitational fields.

## 4. Engine and controls

Preserve the following rules:

- The regular engine accelerates the spacecraft up to 4000.
- Boost allows the engine to accelerate it up to 6000.
- External forces can accelerate the spacecraft beyond both values.
- Disabling boost does not change its existing velocity.
- Once the spacecraft is above the engine's speed limit, the engine must not continue increasing its velocity along the direction of travel.
- Braking and changing direction must remain possible.

Pay particular attention to separating longitudinal and transverse thrust components. Do not disable all spacecraft control when its speed exceeds the engine limit.

Carefully verify how thrust interacts with gravity within a single physics step. The engine limiter must not accidentally cancel the effects of external forces.

## 5. Distortion and the feeling of high speed

I like the current fisheye distortion. Preserve its current quality and character.

Fix its dependency: the effect must be driven by the spacecraft's actual velocity, not by whether the boost button is pressed.

Required behavior:
- At low speeds, distortion is absent or barely noticeable.
- At 6000, retain the current intensity.
- Above 6000, the effect continues to increase, but progressively more slowly.
- At 30000, the effect must still be noticeable, although the increase relative to 6000 should not be enormous.
- Disabling boost must not make the effect disappear while the spacecraft is still moving fast.

As an initial curve to investigate, consider:

`strength = 1 + 0.12 * (1 - exp(-(speed - 6000) / 8000))`

for speeds above 6000.

The current distortion coefficient is `k = 0.30 * fisheyeSmooth`. Preserve its current appearance at 6000, then make further increases smooth and bounded.

This is an artistic function, not a physical formula. Do not tie it directly to the Lorentz factor.

Also fix the disappearance of the star and other objects when distortion is enabled. Check offscreen rendering, transformations, and rendering boundaries.

## 6. Approaching stars, heating, and destruction

The player must be able to approach stars very closely and perform dangerous flybys. Do not instantly destroy the spacecraft when it crosses an outer boundary.

Implement a basic system:
- Gravity continues to affect the spacecraft during approach.
- A danger warning appears.
- Heating accumulates within the corona region.
- Heating depends on distance from the star and exposure duration.
- The spacecraft can cool down after moving away.
- Critical heating or crossing an inner danger boundary causes destruction.

Distances must scale relative to the radius of each individual star.

Add visual feedback: heating, glow, warnings, and gradually intensifying effects.

### Destruction and respawn

A basic respawn equivalent to the initial spacecraft spawn is sufficient for now, but implement a proper destruction sequence:
- Flash.
- Spacecraft destruction.
- Particles and flying debris.
- Effects fading out.
- Spacecraft respawning.

Respawning must not recreate the universe or reset explored planets or other world data. Only reset the spacecraft's state and related temporary effects.

The destruction effect must be independent of the specific spacecraft model.
